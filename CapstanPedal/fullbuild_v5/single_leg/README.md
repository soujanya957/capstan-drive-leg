# One v5 leg for the bench

A single 3-DOF leg from [quadruped v5](../README.md): ab/ad, hip (output B → thigh) and knee (output A → crank → coupler → shin), built on its own and hung on a rail stand. This is how to physically build and test one leg before building four.

## What this is, and how it maps to v5

v5 hangs four [v9.1](../../v9_1_buildable) `leg_abad_gantry` pods in its body. `generate_quad.py` imports v9.1's `generate.py` with `CAPSTAN_LEG_L = 200` and overrides nothing else. So **the turning pod on the bench is the robot's pod, part for part.** Only the frame around it changes:

| | On the robot (v5) | On the bench (this kit) |
|---|---|---|
| Pod: motor plate, posts, wall, drums, gantry, 4-bar leg, end brackets, ab/ad drum and hub | same | same |
| Ab/ad motor C and the hub's two 6808s | in the inner and centre bulkheads | in **Hip_Outer_Plate** and **Hip_Inner_Plate** |
| Front trunnion's 6806 | in the end frame | in **Hip_Front_Plate** |
| What holds it | top and bottom plates | **Hip_Spine** on two MGN12H blocks, on a vertical MGN12 rail on a 2040 stand. The hip slides up and down freely, as on Stanley's single-leg rig |

The bench leg is the **left-front (LF)** pod. The right pods are its mirror image (print the pod parts mirrored). The rear pods use the same parts with the two end brackets swapped. When you build the robot, the four cradle prints (`Hip_Outer/Inner/Front_Plate`, `Hip_Spine`) are bench-only. Everything else goes straight into the quadruped.

## Files

Generated by `make_single_leg.py` (run it in the `capstan-sim` env; `--regen` rebuilds v9.1's exports first):

- `print/` has one STL per printed part, named `<qty>x_<Part>.stl`, in its print orientation (as checked by v9.1's `check_overhangs.py`; your slicer drops it to the bed).
- `step/` holds the same parts as STEP.
- `assembly/leg_abad_gantry.step` is the whole leg on its stand. Open it in Fusion or FreeCAD to see where everything goes.
- `parts.csv` lists each part with its quantity and whether it is a pod part or bench-only.
- `print/0_Fit_Coupon.stl` is a **print-first** part (see below).

## 1. Print the fit coupon first

`0_Fit_Coupon` uses the leg's own allowances (`PRESS` = 0.10 mm on bearing pockets, `JOURNAL_CLR` = 0 on journals). It has:
- a 6806 pocket (Ø42.1, 7 deep). The bearing should press in by hand or with a vice.
- a Ø30 journal stub. The same 6806's inner race should need a light tap to go on.
- an M4 heat-set insert hole (Ø5.6).
- the drums' M4 nylon-nut slot. A DIN 985 nut should slide in from the side and not turn.

If the fits are tight or loose, set `PRESS` / `JOURNAL_CLR` in `../../v9_1_buildable/src/generate.py`, then run `python make_single_leg.py --regen`. Print the leg only after the coupon fits.

## 2. Print the leg (36 parts)

PETG, 0.4 mm nozzle, 4 walls, 40 % infill. Print the **crank in PC** if you can, and the **foot tread in TPU 95A** (3 walls, 20 % gyroid, rim down on the bed). `Hip_Spine` is 312 mm long and fits an H2C bed lengthwise only. The drum colours don't matter; the films use teal (A), orange (B) and violet (ab/ad).

### The foot

The foot is a rigid core in two halves (PETG or PC) clamped on the shin's claw by 2 × M3 × 16 into inserts in half B. A TPU tread snaps over it. The contact radius is still 23 mm, so the sim and the policies don't change. When the tread wears, pull it off and snap on a new one; the core stays on the leg. Print a spare tread or two. See the foot section in `../../v9_1_buildable/README.md`.

## 3. Buy the hardware

**One leg is exactly [v9.1's BOM](../../v9_1_buildable/BOM.md)**, which is written for this build (`leg_abad_gantry`). In short:

- **Motors and drives:** 3 × EaglePower 8308 90 KV (with Ø8 × 2 diametric magnets) and 3 × moteus r4.11.
- **Bearings:** 5 × 6806, 3 × 6808, 3 × 6902, 1 × 6903, 1 × 6000ZZ, 2 × 636ZZ.
- **Fasteners:** 63 heat-set inserts (12 M2.5, 27 M3, 24 M4), plus the screws, pin stacks and the Ø10 × 20 shoulder screw listed in that BOM. Buy a few spare inserts and peg screws.
- **Rope:** about 3.6 m of Ø1.5 mm Dyneema SK99.
- **Rail stand (bench only):**
  - 2040 extrusion: 1 × 900 mm upright, 1 × 500 mm foot, 2 × 300 mm cross feet, plus corner brackets and T-nuts to join them (not in the CAD; any 2040 hardware works).
  - 1 × MGN12 rail, 700 mm, with 2 × MGN12H blocks.
  - 8 × M3 × 14 to fix the blocks to the spine, and 6 × M4 × 16 to fix the spine to the three cradle plates' tabs.
- **Bench electronics** (my suggestion; not in the CAD):
  - A 24 V bench supply (≥ 10 A, current-limited) or the 6S pack.
  - One CAN-FD bus to the three moteus boards, through an mjbots fdcanusb from a laptop or the robot's pi3hat on a Pi 5. On the robot each leg has its own bus.
  - An e-stop in the supply line.

## 4. Build it

Follow the film `../../v9_1_buildable/video/build_leg_abad_gantry.mp4` step by step. **Its order is the build order.** `check_assembly.py` proves that every screw, insert and nut can be reached in that order. Rules from v9.1:
- Press each heat-set insert before its part is closed in. Inserts that a screw pulls on are pressed from the far side.
- Fit the rear end bracket to the ab/ad hub on the bench (8 × M4 × 16 into the hub's inserts) **before** the module goes in. Once the module is in, drum A blocks the hex key.
- Ropes: wind 10 turns on each small drum, and wrap 3 times round each peg's shank before the cross-hole.
- Finally, bolt the MGN12H blocks to the spine, then bolt the spine to the three plates' tabs, and hang the leg on the rail.

## 5. Test it (in this order)

Use v5's joint limits from the start, so the bench matches the robot:

| Joint | Limit |
|---|---|
| Ab/ad | ±12° (v5's software limit; the cradle allows ±20°) |
| Outputs A, B | ±90° (the capstans' travel) |
| Knee | ±60° from rest |
| Output A, in the direction that **pushes** the coupler | **25 N·m** (clip it or set moteus `maximum_torque`). The other direction keeps the full 31.5 N·m |

Gear ratio: 9:1 on each capstan. The moteus encoder is on the rotor, so one output radian is 9 rotor radians.

1. **By hand, unpowered:** move each joint through its full range. Feel for rope slip, rubbing or a tight bearing. Check that the knee stays inside ±60° and that the coupler clears the crank's gantry stub.
2. **Powered, one joint at a time, low gains, current limit at 5 A:** move each joint slowly. Check that the encoder sign and zero match the robot's convention (`../urdf/capstan_quad.urdf`: ab/ad about +X, hip and crank about +Y).
3. **Static torque:** hold 31.5 N·m on each output against a scale on a torque arm. Then check output A's 25 N·m push limit in both directions.
4. **Endurance:** run 1000 crouch-to-stand cycles on the rail. Afterwards check the hub screws' torque, the ropes' tension and play in the pivots.
5. **Drops:** drop the leg 10 cm onto its foot on the rail, then 20 cm.

None of this design has been built yet. Write down what you find, especially the coupon fits, rope stretch and the measured torques. That data should go back into `generate.py` before you print four more legs.
